The first thing I noticed was the silence. Ash drifted through the ruined streets of the city like snow that had forgotten how to fall, and every building I passed wore the same grey coat of cinders. I could not remember my name. I could not remember the face of the person I had been. All I knew was the weight of the sword at my hip, and the strange warmth that spread through my chest whenever I touched its hilt. The sun climbed slowly behind a veil of smoke, staining the world a sickly orange, and somewhere far off a bell began to toll a broken rhythm. I walked toward it. The road was littered with the debris of a thousand small lives, a child's shoe, a shattered music box, a photograph so charred it had become a shadow of itself. I stepped carefully, as if the city were sleeping and I did not want to wake it. And then I saw the other figure standing at the end of the street, hooded and still, watching me with eyes that burned like embers. He raised one hand and pointed, not at me, but past me, toward the burning tower on the hill. I understood, then, that this was not a ruin I had wandered into. It was a beginning.
